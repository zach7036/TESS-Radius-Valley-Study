## 11. Novel contribution

Based on the literature search through 8 August 2026, this study appears to contribute three previously unreported pieces of knowledge:

1. The first located empirical TESS cross-survey test of the reported radius-valley break in adjacent-planet size similarity finds no decisive effect under nominal or probabilistically propagated radii.
2. In the quality-controlled TESS TOI sample, hard 1-sigma interval-overlap labeling inflates valley-inclusive pair labels from 10 to 24 while the measurement model implies 10.33 latent pairs, creating a borderline significant contrast absent from uncertainty propagation.
3. The hard-overlap deficit is stronger, not weaker, among nonadjacent TESS pairs, so the apparent TESS result is not localized to immediate neighbors in the way reported for Kepler.

The precise one-sentence contribution is:

> Previous Kepler research established a loss of physical size-similarity enhancement for pairs adjacent to radius-valley planets, but an independent TESS test and the behavior of the interval-overlap membership rule remained unknown. Using 74 quality-controlled TESS adjacent pairs, graph-preserving permutation, system bootstrap, latent-radius simulation, and nonadjacent placebos, this study found that nominal TESS evidence is inconclusive and that the only borderline apparent replication is generated by a hard uncertainty-overlap rule that labels 24 pairs despite an expected latent count of 10.33 and fails adjacency localization.

## 12. Scientific significance

The result is **incremental but meaningful**, not breakthrough-level.

Its astrophysical contribution is a qualified negative replication: it prevents current TESS catalog evidence from being counted as independent confirmation of a potentially important Kepler result. Its methodological contribution is broader: it gives a concrete, quantified example in which a common interval-overlap classification rule reverses the normal meaning of uncertainty and overstates evidence in a cross-survey analysis.

The study moves the frontier by narrowing what can currently be claimed, specifying the failure mechanism mathematically, quantifying its magnitude in real public data, and identifying the data quality needed for a decisive replication. It does not overturn the peas-in-a-pod literature, disprove the Kepler finding, or establish a new planet-formation mechanism.

## 13. Reproducibility

### 13.1 Repository files

This GitHub repository includes:

- `download_data.py` - downloads the public MAST TOI catalog and verifies whether its SHA-256 matches the exact snapshot used in the study.
- `analysis_parts/` plus `tess_valley_analysis.py` - the complete analysis pipeline, stored in executable source parts and run through a small launcher.
- `independent_validation.py` - separate primary-count and latent-count validation.
- `summary.json` - machine-readable result summary.
- `tables/` - the core cleaned pair-level tables, classifications, robustness grid, leave-one-system-out results, center scan, and power curves.
- `figures/` - all five report figures in vector SVG format.
- `live_endpoint_check.json` - endpoint URL, check date, headers, and checksum comparison.
- `search_log.md` - literature and novelty-search record.
- `requirements.txt` - tested package versions.

The original large Monte Carlo/permutation draw files are deterministically regenerated by the included pipeline from the fixed seed rather than being required in version control.

### 13.2 Exact command

First acquire the public input:

```bash
python download_data.py
```

```text
6797d7ea49dedd95cf5a5711fe4c93553f218268d3ce68f9fbde569d8ff06a7e
```

```bash
python tess_valley_analysis.py \
```
