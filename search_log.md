# Literature and novelty search log

**Search date:** 8 August 2026

## Candidate screen

1. General TESS peas-in-a-pod behavior - rejected because Otegi, Helled & Bouchy (2022) already analyzed general similarity in radii, masses, densities, and spacing in a TESS-follow-up-enriched sample.
2. Uniformity below versus above the valley - rejected because Goyal & Wang (2024) compared wholly rocky and wholly volatile-rich systems.
3. In-valley planets versus immediate neighbors in Kepler - rejected as a discovery target because Chance & Ballard (arXiv v2, 4 June 2026; AJ DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/ae77ec) directly answered it in Kepler.
4. TESS M-dwarf-only version - rejected as a standalone definitive study because the primary sample contained only 11 adjacent M-dwarf pairs.
5. Independent TESS replication plus uncertainty-rule analysis - selected because no prior empirical TESS replication or quantification of interval-overlap label inflation was located and the complete analysis was executable with public data.

## Closest located studies

- Weiss et al. (2018): Kepler peas-in-a-pod radius and spacing pattern.
- Weiss & Petigura (2020): evidence the Kepler pattern is astrophysical.
- Otegi et al. (2022): general multi-planet similarity using a sample enabled by TESS follow-up.
- Goyal & Wang (2024): systems entirely below versus entirely above the valley.
- Chance & Ballard (2026): Kepler adjacent pairs containing in-valley planets; difference-in-differences p = 0.001 in revised analysis.
- Parashivamurthy & Mulders (2025): TESS M/K/G radius-valley locations used here.

## Post-result novelty assessment

No located paper reported all or any of the following as an empirical TESS result:

- a direct cross-survey test of the Chance-Ballard adjacent-pair finding;
- 2.4-fold inflation of TESS valley-pair hard labels under a 1-sigma interval-overlap rule;
- comparison of those hard labels with an expected latent pair count from the reported uncertainties;
- a stronger hard-overlap deficit among nonadjacent than adjacent TESS pairs;
- disappearance of the borderline hard-label result under direct latent-radius propagation.

The wording in the paper is deliberately cautious: the contribution *appears previously unreported* after the searches above. A search cannot prove that no obscure or unindexed work exists.
